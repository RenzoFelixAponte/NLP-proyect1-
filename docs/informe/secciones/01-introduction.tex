\section{Introduction}

BERT \citep{devlin2019bert} made pre-training followed by fine-tuning the
default recipe for text classification. Its cost shows up at inference, where
a deployed model spends most of its life and every request crosses 12
transformer layers. DistilBERT \citep{sanh2019distilbert} halves the depth
through knowledge distillation and reports 97\% of BERT's GLUE score.

We set one rule for this project: the model is the only thing allowed to
differ between two columns of any table, so both networks share the training
loop, splits and seed. The rule broke once, for latency. The per-run timing logged after each
fine-tuning claimed that BERT answered faster than DistilBERT on SST-2
(3.16 against 3.50\,ms), which a network with twice the layers cannot do. We
threw those numbers away and measured both models again on a fixed grid
(Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}).

We fine-tune both models on SST-2 \citep{socher2013sst,wang2019glue},
AG News and Yelp Polarity \citep{zhang2015character} and report accuracy,
macro precision, recall and F1, along with parameters, latency and GPU
memory. An ablation on DistilBERT varies the classifier head and freezes the
encoder, and the best configuration is re-run on BERT with every metric.
